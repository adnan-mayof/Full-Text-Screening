# Step 9 — Full-Text Screening

[← Previous Step: Title and Abstract Screening](https://github.com/adnan-mayof/Title-and-Abstract-Screening/blob/main/README.md)

---

## 🧭 Maya Moves to Full-Text Screening

Maya has finished screening the titles and abstracts in Rayyan.

She excluded records that clearly did not meet her eligibility criteria and retained records that appeared potentially eligible.

Now she has a smaller set of records that need further examination.

> **Maya:** “I finished the title and abstract screening. What happens next?”

> **Mentor:** “Now we need to retrieve and examine the **full text** of the potentially eligible records.”

> **Maya:** “Is this where I make the final decision about whether a study belongs in my review?”

> **Mentor:** “Exactly. Full-text screening is where you determine whether each potentially eligible report meets all of your eligibility criteria.”

---

# 1. Retrieve the Full Text

Maya begins by finding the full text of each potentially eligible record.

She may obtain the full text through:

* Her university library
* The publisher
* Open-access repositories
* Institutional repositories
* Author-shared versions
* Other legitimate sources

Maya keeps track of which full texts she has obtained.

Her workflow is now:

```text
Title and Abstract Screening
          ↓
Potentially Eligible Records
          ↓
Retrieve Full Text
          ↓
Full-Text Screening
```

> **Mentor:** “Try to retrieve the full text for every record that you retained after title and abstract screening.”

---

# 2. What If Maya Cannot Find the Full Text?

Maya encounters a problem.

She cannot find the full text of one study.

> **Maya:** “I found the citation, but I can't access the full article. Should I exclude it?”

> **Mentor:** “Not immediately. First, document your attempts to retrieve it.”

Maya checks the available sources specified by her review workflow.

For example:

```text
Publisher
    ↓
University Library
    ↓
Institutional Repository
    ↓
Open-Access Source
    ↓
Other Appropriate Retrieval Route
```

If the full text still cannot be obtained, Maya records that information.

She should use the retrieval or exclusion category specified in her protocol rather than simply treating the study as irrelevant.

> **Mentor:** “Remember, **full text unavailable** is different from **the study does not meet the eligibility criteria**.”

---

# 3. Read the Full Text Against the Eligibility Criteria

Maya now has the full article.

She reads the study carefully and checks it against every relevant eligibility criterion.

For example:

| Criterion            | Question Maya Asks                                                        |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Population           | Does the study include the population specified in the protocol?          |
| Intervention         | Does it examine the AI-powered learning technology defined in the review? |
| Comparison           | Does it include the required comparison condition?                        |
| Outcome              | Does it report a relevant learning outcome?                               |
| Study design         | Does it use an eligible study design?                                     |
| Publication criteria | Does it meet the predefined publication criteria?                         |
| Other criteria       | Does it meet any additional eligibility requirements?                     |

> **Mentor:** “Don't rely only on the abstract now. Use the full text to make the eligibility decision.”

---

# 4. Why Full-Text Screening Is Different From Title and Abstract Screening

Maya asks:

> **Maya:** “Why do we need another screening stage? Didn't I already screen these records?”

> **Mentor:** “The two stages have different purposes.”

### Title and Abstract Screening

Maya asks:

> **Could this record potentially be eligible?**

She works with limited information.

### Full-Text Screening

Maya asks:

> **Does this study actually meet all of my eligibility criteria?**

She now has much more information.

```text
Title + Abstract
      ↓
Potential Eligibility
      ↓
Full Text
      ↓
Final Eligibility Decision
```

---

# 5. Apply the Eligibility Criteria Consistently

Maya should apply the same criteria to every full-text record.

For example, suppose her protocol requires:

* Higher education students
* AI-powered learning technology
* A comparison group
* Learning outcomes
* Experimental or quasi-experimental design

Maya finds a study about AI and student learning, but the participants are **K–12 students**.

The study may be highly relevant to the topic, but it does not meet Maya's population criterion.

She records:

> **Population does not meet eligibility criteria**

The important point is:

> **A study can be highly relevant to the topic but still not meet the eligibility criteria for a particular systematic review.**

---

# 6. Record a Specific Reason for Exclusion

Maya should record **why** an eligible-looking full-text study was excluded.

Possible reasons include:

```text
Population does not meet eligibility criteria
Intervention does not meet eligibility criteria
Comparison does not meet eligibility criteria
Outcome does not meet eligibility criteria
Study design does not meet eligibility criteria
Publication type does not meet eligibility criteria
Language criterion not met
Required information unavailable
Duplicate or related report
Full text unavailable
Other protocol-defined reason
```

The exact exclusion categories should reflect Maya's protocol.

> **Mentor:** “Don't simply write ‘not relevant.’ Record a specific reason that connects to your eligibility criteria.”

For example:

❌ **Not relevant**

Better:

✅ **Population does not meet eligibility criteria — participants were K–12 students, but the review includes higher education students.**

---

# 7. What If a Study Has More Than One Reason for Exclusion?

Maya encounters a study that has a population that does not meet her eligibility criteria and also uses a study design that does not meet her criteria.

> **Maya:** “Which reason should I record?”

> **Mentor:** “Follow the exclusion-reason rule you established for your review.”

For example, Maya may record the **first or primary reason** identified according to her predefined screening procedure.

The important thing is to use the same approach consistently.

> **Mentor:** “Don't change your rule from one study to another.”

---

# 8. What If the Full Text Provides New Information?

Sometimes the title and abstract do not provide enough information to make a final decision.

For example, the abstract might say:

> “Students participated in an AI-supported learning intervention.”

That sounds eligible.

But the full text reveals that the participants were actually **high school students**.

Maya now has enough information to determine that the study does not meet her higher-education population criterion.

She records:

> **Population does not meet eligibility criteria**

This is why full-text screening is necessary.

---

# 9. What If Two Reports Come From the Same Study?

Maya encounters another important situation.

She finds two publications that appear to report results from the same underlying study.

> **Maya:** “These are two different papers, but they seem to use the same participants. Should I count them as two studies?”

> **Mentor:** “Not necessarily.”

A single study can produce multiple reports.

For example:

```text
One Study
    ├── Journal Article
    ├── Conference Paper
    └── Follow-up Report
```

Maya should determine whether the publications represent:

* The same study
* Different studies
* Different reports from the same study
* A follow-up using the same or overlapping participants

This distinction becomes especially important when preparing data for meta-analysis.

> **Mentor:** “Your unit of analysis is important. Multiple reports do not automatically mean multiple independent studies.”

---

# 10. What If a Full Text Is in Another Language?

Maya finds a potentially eligible article written in another language.

> **Maya:** “What should I do now?”

> **Mentor:** “Go back to the language criteria in your protocol.”

If the protocol specifies language restrictions, Maya applies those criteria.

If the protocol does not exclude the language, she should follow the planned approach for handling non-English or other-language studies.

The key principle remains:

> **Apply the protocol consistently rather than creating a new exclusion rule during screening.**

---

# 11. What If the Full Text Does Not Report Enough Information?

Maya finds an article that appears eligible, but the full text does not clearly report one of the required pieces of information.

For example, the study appears to examine an AI learning intervention, but Maya cannot determine whether the outcome is one of the outcomes specified in her protocol.

She does not automatically assume that the study is eligible or ineligible.

She follows the procedure established in her protocol.

Depending on the review plan, this may involve:

* Looking for supplementary information
* Checking another report from the same study
* Contacting the authors, if planned
* Recording insufficient information
* Excluding the study if the required eligibility information cannot be established

---

# 13. Maya's Full-Text Screening Workflow

Maya summarizes the process:

```text
Potentially Eligible Records
          ↓
Retrieve Full Text
          ↓
Full Text Available?
      ↙           ↘
    Yes            No
     ↓              ↓
Read Full Text   Document Retrieval Attempts
     ↓              ↓
Check Eligibility Criteria
          ↓
   ┌──────┴──────┐
   ↓             ↓
Eligible      Does Not Meet
              Eligibility Criteria
   ↓             ↓
Include       Record Specific
              Exclusion Reason
   ↓
Final Set of Eligible Studies
```

If two reports may come from the same study:

```text
Compare Reports
      ↓
Authors / Participants / Intervention /
Setting / Dates / Study Characteristics
      ↓
Same Underlying Study?
      ↓
Handle According to Review Protocol
```

# 14. Maya's Screening Record

Maya now has a clear record of what happened at each stage.

```text
Records Retrieved
        ↓
Duplicates Removed
        ↓
Title/Abstract Screening
        ↓
Records Excluded
        ↓
Full Texts Sought
        ↓
Full Texts Assessed
        ↓
Full Texts Excluded + Reasons
        ↓
Studies Included
```

> **Mentor:** “This record is important because your final review needs to show how you moved from the original search results to the studies that were ultimately included.”

---

# ⭐ Important Principle

> **Full-text screening is the stage where Maya makes the final eligibility decision for potentially relevant records. She retrieves and reads the full text, applies the eligibility criteria defined in the protocol, records a specific reason when a study is excluded, and documents records whose full text cannot be obtained. She should also distinguish between multiple reports and independent studies so that the same underlying study is not incorrectly treated as multiple independent studies.**
